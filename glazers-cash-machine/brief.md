## Voiceover narration

### 1 — The machine

```text
[Controlled anger. Low, direct opening; dry contempt on “business model”.]

Manchester United’s greatest strength has always been its supporters..... Under the Glazers, that loyalty became a business model..... We buy the tickets. We buy the shirts. We bring our kids up loving the club..... And while we’re waiting for United to be rebuilt, the family have found several ways to get paid..... Dividends. Selling shares. Keeping control..... They didn’t need to sell the whole cow..... They could keep selling the milk.
```

Producer source note (do not narrate): Editorial argument; factual mechanisms established in S1–S11.

### 2 — Buying United with United’s future

```text
[Clear explanation, building disbelief. Hit “own takeover” firmly.]

Go back to 2005..... The takeover valued United at around seven hundred and ninety million pounds. Much of it was borrowed..... The ownership structure left the club carrying the cost of the deal..... Think about that. United’s future income was being used to service the borrowing behind its own takeover..... Imagine buying somebody’s house, then making the people already living there cover the financing..... They get the keys. You get the bills..... And somehow, you’re supposed to thank them for moving in.
```

Producer source note (do not narrate): S1. The house comparison is a metaphor for acquisition financing.

### 3 — What the club lost

```text
[Measured, forensic delivery for the statistic. Pause, then rising anger.]

BBC Verify calculated £1.187 billion left United in takeover-related interest, debt repayments, dividends and family-company fees between 2005 and 2024..... That wasn’t a transfer budget. It wasn’t a new stand..... And it wasn’t all money paid to the Glazers personally: lenders received interest and repayments..... But it was money leaving the club through the financial structure around their ownership..... Football has plenty of expensive mistakes..... United have been paying for the privilege of being bought.
```

Producer source note (do not narrate): S4. The following explanation distinguishes lenders from shareholders; do not display the total as the family's personal income.

### 4 — Failure with a payout

```text
[Disbelief; sarcastic lift on “excellent year”; let the last line land.]

Look at 2021–22..... United finished sixth. Solskjaer was sacked. Rangnick’s spell unravelled..... The accounts show thirty-three point six million pounds paid in shareholder dividends that financial year..... Those payments went to shareholders, with the family receiving the majority..... So while supporters watched another season fall apart, ownership still came with a payout..... An excellent year, apparently..... Provided you judged it by the money leaving Old Trafford rather than the football being played inside it.
```

Producer source note (do not narrate): S2 (dividend discussion and Note 12); S13 (season context).

### 5 — Selling pieces of the club

```text
[Brisk, investigative tone. Slow sharply for “the club received nothing”.]

Then there were the share sales..... In 2021, Avram sold roughly seventy million pounds’ worth. Kevin and Edward’s sale brought them about a hundred and seventeen million..... In those transactions, the club received nothing..... The buyers paid the selling owners..... That distinction matters. Selling your shares can make you richer without putting a penny into the team..... So when someone tells you United attracted investment, ask where the money actually landed..... In the club’s bank account..... Or the owners’?
```

Producer source note (do not narrate): S5 and S6. These are examples within historic share-sale totals, not extra sums to add on top.

### 6 — The Ratcliffe payday

```text
[Dry sarcasm at the start; precise on the distinction; contempt at the end.]

And then came the supposed fresh start..... The Ratcliffe deal..... Reporting at the time estimated around seven hundred and fifteen million pounds for the Glazers’ sale of a quarter of their B shares..... Earlier reporting put previous share-sale receipts at about four hundred and sixty-five million, and family dividends at around a hundred and fifty million..... Together, roughly one point three three billion pounds in gross receipts, including that deal..... Separate from that, Ratcliffe committed three hundred million dollars of new money to the club..... One payment bought shares from owners. The other funded United..... They are different things..... Yet somehow a story about rescuing Manchester United also became another enormous payday for the family who had owned it all along.
```

Producer source note (do not narrate): S7 for contemporary sterling estimates; S8 and S11 for separate club equity funding. Gross receipts are not net profit.

### 7 — Cash out, keep control

```text
[Forensic, then incredulous. Emphasise “voting power”, not just the percentage.]

Here’s the detail that makes it worse..... The 2026 annual report still puts the Glazer family at about sixty-eight per cent of the voting power..... Their B shares carry ten votes each. An A share carries one..... INEOS runs football operations under the agreement, with its own rights and responsibilities..... But the family retained corporate voting control..... Sell slices. Collect the proceeds. Keep the decisive shareholder vote..... For supporters, change keeps arriving with the same owners still sitting above it.
```

Producer source note (do not narrate): S9, shareholder discussion and Item 7; S8 for delegated football operations.

### 8 — The bill is still running

```text
[Sober and exact on figures; anger returns after the pause.]

And the bill hasn’t stopped..... At June 2026, United had roughly six hundred and eighty-nine million pounds in borrowings, plus three hundred and seventy-five million in transfer-related payables..... That’s over a billion combined, although those are different obligations..... The club paid about thirty-eight point eight million pounds in cash interest during that financial year..... No dividend was paid that year..... But suspending the dividend doesn’t erase the borrowing..... The meter is still running while supporters wait for the club their loyalty deserves.
```

Producer source note (do not narrate): S10, balance sheet and cash flow; S9, Note 24. Includes associated acquisition costs within transfer payables.

### 9 — Our club, their asset

```text
[Personal, hurt, then firm. Build without shouting; final line quiet and deliberate.]

Yes, United have spent heavily on players..... Money badly spent is part of the failure too. It doesn’t make the financing costs disappear..... And improvements now don’t give twenty years of ownership a free pass..... The question is what United could have built with more of its own money staying in the club..... Better decisions. Better facilities. A stronger future..... We support United because it means something to us..... They’ve been able to profit from the fact we can’t simply stop caring..... That’s what makes the cash-cow criticism so damning..... Our loyalty keeps the business valuable..... We deserve owners who make the football worthy of it.
```

Producer source note (do not narrate): Editorial judgement. S12 establishes recent facility investment; avoid claiming nothing has ever been improved.

## Additional detail that strengthens this version

The structural point is that a family's economic shareholding can fall while enhanced voting rights preserve shareholder control. Separate club earnings, owner equity, and payments for existing shares. Also distinguish a cash-interest outflow from net finance costs, which can include foreign-exchange movements. Our interest line uses cash actually paid, not the larger net-finance-cost headline. Recent Carrington investment occurred; the argument is the long-term allocation of money and quality of stewardship, not a false claim that no facility has ever changed. [S8–S12]

There are three distinct ledgers: (1) cash leaving United, (2) gross receipts reaching owners from dividends and sales, and (3) value of shares they still hold. They overlap and cannot simply be added into one 'stolen from the club' total. The force of the criticism comes from the financial incentives and choices, not an allegation of theft.
